About

Currently, I am a Ph.D. candidate in the Department of Physics at Carnegie Mellon University, advised by Prof. Jyoti Katoch in the Lab for Investigating Quantum Materials, Interfaces and Devices (LIQUID). My current research focus lies within low dimensional, correlated systems.

To explore these materials, I primarily use angle resolved photoemission spectroscopy (ARPES) to visualize their momentum resolved spectral function at the nanoscale. In addition, X-ray magnetic dichroism photoemission electron microscopy (X-PEEM) is employed for studying emergent magnetic phenomena in the 2D limit. These experiments are performed at leading international synchrotron facilities, including the Advanced Light Source (ALS) (USA), BESSY II (Germany), Synchrotron SOLEIL (France), ALBA (Spain), and NSLS-II (USA). Afterward, a combination of statistical modelling and machine-learning methods is used for the analysis of multidimensional datasets generated during beamtimes. The extracted physical insights are discussed in strong collaboration with condensed matter theorists.

To prepare for these measurements, cleanroom nanofabrication and controlled glovebox environments are utilized for the van der Waals assembly of devices. Alongside this, in-house atomic force microscopy (AFM), Raman spectroscopy, and transport setups are employed for preliminary structural and electronic characterization.

Prior to this, my foundations in experimental physics began at IISER Kolkata, India, where I earned an integrated BS–MS in Physics. I conducted research at the National Centre of High Pressure Studies under Prof. Goutam Dev Mukherjee, and in the Advanced Functional Materials Lab with Prof. Sayan Bhattacharyya. My Master's project involved studying structural and vibrational properties through low temperature and high pressure Raman spectroscopy, four probe transport, X-ray diffraction (XRD), and infrared spectroscopy on a synthesised superconducting cuprate and a vacancy ordered perovskite halide.
