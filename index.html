About

I am a Ph.D. candidate in the Department of Physics at Carnegie Mellon University, advised by Prof. Jyoti Katoch in the Lab for Investigating Quantum Materials, Interfaces and Devices (LIQUID). My research focuses on low dimensional, correlated systems.

To explore the fundamental physics governing these systems, my primary probe is angle resolved photoemission spectroscopy (ARPES), which directly visualizes their momentum resolved spectral function at the nanoscale. By mapping the electronic band structure, it provides direct access to many body interactions and quasiparticle dynamics. Further, for magnetic systems, X-ray photoemission electron microscopy (X-PEEM) is used to reveal emergent magnetic phenomena in the 2D limit, for potential spintronics applications.

Moreover, I combine statistical modeling and machine learning to extract physical insights from massive beamtime datasets.

To prepare for these measurements, I use cleanroom nanofabrication and controlled glovebox environments to assemble van der Waals devices, then perform preliminary structural and electronic characterization with atomic force microscopy (AFM), Raman spectroscopy, and transport setups.

Because these techniques require tunable electromagnetic sources, I perform these experiments at leading international synchrotron facilities, including the Advanced Light Source (ALS) (USA), BESSY II (Germany), Synchrotron SOLEIL (France), ALBA (Spain), and NSLS-II (USA).

My foundations in experimental physics began at IISER Kolkata, India, where I earned an integrated BS–MS in Physics. My master's project examined the structural and vibrational properties of solids using low temperature and high pressure Raman spectroscopy, four probe transport, X-ray diffraction (XRD), and infrared spectroscopy — work carried out mainly at the National Centre of High Pressure Studies under Prof. Goutam Dev Mukherjee, together with the Advanced Functional Materials Lab of Prof. Sayan Bhattacharyya, where the synthesis focused on a cuprate superconductor and a vacancy-ordered perovskite halide.
