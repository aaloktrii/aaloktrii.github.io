Biography

I am a Ph.D. candidate in the Department of Physics at Carnegie Mellon University, advised by Prof. Jyoti Katoch in the Lab for Investigating Quantum Materials, Interfaces and Devices (LIQUID). My research focuses on low-dimensional, correlated systems.

My primary probes are angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy (ARPES), which directly visualizes the momentum-resolved spectral function at the nanoscale, and X-ray photoemission electron microscopy (X-PEEM), used to image emergent magnetic order in the 2D limit for potential spintronics applications. I combine these with cleanroom nanofabrication to assemble van der Waals devices, and with statistical modeling and machine learning to extract physical insights from large beamtime datasets. Experiments are carried out at leading international synchrotron facilities: ALS (USA), BNL (USA), BESSY II (Germany), Synchrotron SOLEIL (France), and ALBA (Spain).

I received an integrated BS–MS in Physics from IISER Kolkata, India, where my master’s project examined the structural and vibrational properties of solids under low temperature and high pressure at the National Centre of High Pressure Studies (Prof. G. D. Mukherjee) and the Advanced Functional Materials Lab (Prof. S. Bhattacharyya).

Research Interests

  • Electronic structure of 2D quantum materials — momentum-resolved band mapping with nano- and micro-ARPES; many-body interactions and quasiparticle dynamics in monolayer and few-layer devices.
  • Magnetism at the nanoscale — element-specific imaging of magnetic order in atomically thin and twisted van der Waals magnets; current-driven domain manipulation and spintronic device physics via X-PEEM.
  • Van der Waals heterostructures and field-effect devices — cleanroom assembly, in-situ perturbation (strain, gate, magnetic field), and surface preparation from inert to UHV environments.
  • AI-assisted photoemission analysis — physics-aware machine learning and statistical modeling for multidimensional ARPES datasets.
  • High-pressure and low-temperature spectroscopy — Raman, photoluminescence, and transport across structural transitions in quantum materials and energy-relevant compounds.
