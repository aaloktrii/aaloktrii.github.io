Research

I use synchrotron spectroscopy and microscopy to see, at the momentum and nanometer scale, how electrons and spins organize themselves in atomically thin materials — and I build the devices and grow the materials that make those measurements possible.

Electronic structure of 2D quantum materials

Using nano- and micro-ARPES, I map the momentum-resolved band structure of monolayer and few-layer materials. This includes realizing the first momentum-resolved electronic band structure of monolayer TaIrTe4, a candidate Weyl semimetal, and probing how stacking and substrates reshape the electronic states of graphene-based heterostructures.

Nanoscale magnetic imaging of van der Waals magnets

With XMCD- and XMLD-PEEM, I image magnetic order in atomically thin antiferromagnets such as CrSBr, resolving how layer parity, twist angle, encapsulation, temperature, and applied field control the spin texture at the nanoscale — directly relevant to 2D spintronic and memory devices.

Spectroscopy of functional van der Waals devices

I fabricate van der Waals heterostructures and devices in the clean room and study them in operando with focused photoemission, connecting the intrinsic electronic structure of a material to its behavior in a working device.

High-pressure & vibrational studies of quantum and energy materials

I synthesize materials — from Cs2TiCl6 double perovskites to the cuprate superconductor YBCO — and probe their lattice dynamics with temperature-dependent (4–273 K) and high-pressure (to 30 GPa, diamond anvil cell) Raman spectroscopy, extracting phonon anharmonicity and phase behavior.

Methods & instrumentation

Much of my work lives at the instrument: operating, aligning, and troubleshooting ultra-high-vacuum photoemission endstations (base pressures down to ~10−11 mbar), and building Python/MATLAB pipelines that turn large, noisy spectroscopic datasets into quantitative physical conclusions.
